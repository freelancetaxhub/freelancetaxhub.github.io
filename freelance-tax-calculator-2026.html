What Is Self-Employment Tax?

Self-employment tax is a federal tax that freelancers and independent contractors pay to cover their Social Security and Medicare contributions. Unlike W-2 employees who split this tax with their employer, self-employed individuals must pay the full 15.3% themselves.

This rate breaks down as:

  • 12.4% for Social Security (on income up to $168,600 in 2026)
  • 2.9% for Medicare (no income limit)
  • 0.9% additional Medicare tax for incomes above $200,000

Frequently Asked Questions

How much tax do freelancers pay in the USA?

Freelancers typically pay between 25% and 35% of their net income in combined self-employment and federal income tax. The exact rate depends on income level, deductions, and filing status.

Do I need to pay quarterly taxes as a freelancer?

Yes, if you expect to owe at least $1,000 in taxes for the year. Quarterly payments help you avoid IRS underpayment penalties.

What is the self-employment tax rate in 2026?

The self-employment tax rate is 15.3%, which includes 12.4% for Social Security and 2.9% for Medicare.

Can I deduct my home office as a freelancer?

Yes. If you use part of your home exclusively for business, you can deduct $5 per square foot up to 300 square feet using the simplified method.

How accurate is this freelance tax calculator?

This calculator provides a reliable estimate based on 2026 IRS tax brackets and standard self-employment tax rates. For exact figures, consult a licensed tax professional.

What is the difference between 1099 and W-2 income?

1099 income is earned as an independent contractor with no taxes withheld. W-2 income comes from traditional employment where taxes are automatically withheld by the employer.

Are freelancers eligible for the standard deduction?

Yes. In 2026, single freelancers can claim a $14,600 standard deduction and married couples filing jointly can claim $29,200.

Pro Tax Tips for Freelancers in 2026

  • Set aside 25–30% of every payment for taxes
  • Open a separate business bank account
  • Use accounting software to track expenses
  • Make quarterly tax payments on time
  • Contribute to a SEP-IRA to reduce taxable income
  • Keep digital receipts for all business expenses
  • Hire a CPA if your income exceeds $100,000
